Medical drones only pay off where both hospitals sit on the water.

New York's first medical drone route crosses the East River in about four minutes. I modeled what happens on the rest of the trip, hospital door to hospital door, and asked where a second route should go under today's over-water rule.

Executive summary

Under the over-water rule, drones work as a proof of concept, not yet as a business.

01

The river is not the bottleneck. The land legs are.

The trial route crosses in minutes, but its Brooklyn pad is 2.1 miles from the nearest same-system hospital. Hospital to hospital, it is 5 to 8 minutes slower than a car at every hour it operates.

02

Pad location decides whether drones beat the road.

Put both pads beside waterfront hospitals and the same drone wins by 14 minutes in the morning and 26 in the evening rush, and the drone leg can cost up to $106 a trip, not $30, before driving is cheaper.

03

The market is small until drones can fly inland.

Of four candidate hospital pairs screened, one has both ends on the water. Allow over-land flight and both routes beat the road by 8 to 26 minutes. The business case sits in the next rule change, not in this one.

Recommendation

Site the second route between waterfront hospitals of one health system, fly it into the evening rush, and first run a two-week test to measure long-route flight time, the one number that can flip this result.

Situation

NYC is moving freight onto the water and into the air.

The Port Authority and Skyports fly light medical cargo between Downtown Skyport and Brooklyn Marine Terminal, weekdays 9 to 5, through April 2027.

Complication

Both pads are on the water. Most hospitals are not.

Drones may only fly over water today, so every trip starts and ends with a ground leg through Manhattan and Brooklyn traffic.

Question

Where should the second route go?

Which hospital pairs, at which hours, actually beat a car on time and cost under current rules?

Answer

Between waterfront hospitals, at rush hour.

Value is real but narrow. Scale depends on over-land flight being allowed.

Two routes compared

The trial route leaves 2.7 miles of ground travel. A waterfront pair leaves 0.6.

Both routes link two hospitals of the same health system, where lab and pharmacy shipments already move. Drones fly only over water; everything else is a car or cargo bike.

Unit economics

Moving the pads raises what a drone leg can cost from $30 to $106 a trip.

The operator's cost per flight is not public, so I solved for the break-even: the most the drone leg can cost before driving the whole way is cheaper. A credible drone leg should be able to cost at least as much as the cheapest Manhattan STAT courier run, $65.

Why it is not a business yet

Geography caps the market under the over-water rule.

A per-trip break-even of $106 only matters with volume. Pads, staff and aircraft are fixed costs, and very few hospital pairs sit on the water at both ends. A handful of rush-hour trips cannot carry them. That matches how the operator describes the trial: roughly break-even.

Two more limits stack on top: the trial does not fly in the evening rush, and both Manhattan pads share airspace with helicopters landing below 1,000 feet.

What would make it work

Three moves, cheapest first.

  1. Measure the long route. A two-week test on E 34th St ↔ Sunset Park settles the flight time that decides route B.
  2. Fly the rush. Extend operations to 5–7 pm, where the road is slowest and the gain is largest.
  3. Open inland corridors. Over-land approval converts a niche into a network: both modeled routes would beat the road by 8 to 26 minutes, with no ground legs at all. First customer: a health system with waterfront campuses on both sides, such as NYU Langone.

Method and assumptions

How each trip is built

leg time = straight-line distance × 1.35 ÷ speed + stop time

cost = (labor + vehicle + tolls) × 1.30

drone break-even = road cost − ground-leg cost

Each mode is a chain of legs: car or cargo bike to the pad, drone over water, ground leg to the destination. Splitting legs shows exactly where time is gained and lost. Port + drone uses whichever of car or bike is faster for each ground leg.
